# Next work after rejected fan-in trial 064

The wider merge reduces maintenance count from seven to four, and improves reopening the small empty-history store, but fails five of six native speed goals plus one maximum guard. Identical controls fail five of eighteen checks. Do not promote it or run service qualification contrary to its declared advance policy. Keep the source, all raw rounds and full stores.

The remaining write bottleneck deserves a narrower profile on exact released source. At 99,000 receipts, catalog encoding is 18.33% of A's summed stage time in this trial; at 50,000 it is 14.49%. Synchronization and occasional long stalls remain. These sums include maintenance and are not p99 causes. Historical CAT6 and file-sizing failures stay visible, so another format change or reservation scheme should not be repeated without new evidence.

A useful next step is an isolated, allocation-aware profile of the unchanged catalog encoder and validator using the preserved 50,000/99,000 receipt stores. Look for avoidable cloning, intermediate JSON allocation or repeated validation while preserving serialized bytes, receipt generations and durability. Predeclare equal-output checks, A/A calibration and the smallest justified experiment before changing code. Any new speed candidate must pass full correctness/crash/source replay qualification and native guards before service comparisons.

Keep improving data-engineering usability where it has a demonstrated need: reproducible recovery inspection and exact source-position/retry diagnostics. New comparative claims require fresh equivalent workloads.
